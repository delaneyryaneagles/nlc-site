Historic District Guide

Replacing Windows & Doors in Historic Districts (HARB & HPC)

Town by town, what the historic boards ask for on windows, doors and porches where we work.

Old house, new windows. Check the street first.

Updated November 16, 2026

If your house sits in a historic district, the windows, doors and porch on the front of it aren't only your call. A review board gets a say, and some towns won't issue a building permit until that board signs off.

Below are the rules we've looked up for the towns we work in, sorted by state. If your town isn't listed, call us and we'll check it for you.

We're licensed in all three states: PA HIC #PA201433, DE #2026700487 and NJ HIC #13VH14325000. Family-run, over 50 years in the trade, and fully insured.

How historic review works.

Is your address in a district?

Review only applies inside a town's historic district or overlay. Radnor, Lower Merion, Media, Chadds Ford, Birmingham, Haddonfield, Moorestown and Mullica Hill each have mapped districts, and New Castle County has historic overlay parcels.

Who reviews it

In Pennsylvania it's usually a Historical Architectural Review Board (HARB) advising the township or borough. In New Jersey it's a Historic Preservation Commission (HPC). New Castle County uses a Historic Review Board.

The Certificate of Appropriateness

The approval is called a Certificate of Appropriateness. In Chadds Ford and Birmingham it comes before the building permit, and New Castle County needs Historic Review Board approval before any exterior permit.

What gets reviewed

Exterior work. In Media it's changes visible from the street; in Lower Merion, visible exterior changes; in Haddonfield, 'any alteration to the exterior appearance,' with paint color and landscaping exempt. Interior work isn't reviewed by Haddonfield's HPC.

Pennsylvania

Radnor Township (Wayne)

North Wayne, South Wayne and Louella Court are historic districts. Exterior work there, including porches, windows and doors, goes through Radnor's Historical Architectural Review Board (HARB).

HARB meets the first Wednesday of the month. We submit drawings and photos and attend for you.

On houses like these we match what's there: true divided-lite windows where they belong, and porch columns and rails milled to the original profiles.

Lower Merion Township

Seven local historic districts: Ardmore Commercial Center, Merion Friends Meeting/General Wayne Inn, Haverford Station, English Village, Harriton, Gladwyne/Merion Square and Mill Creek.

HARB reviews visible exterior changes in those districts, and we prepare the submission.

Media Borough

Three local historic districts: Courthouse Square, Lemon Street and Providence Friends' Meeting House.

In those districts, exterior changes visible from the street need a Certificate of Appropriateness, issued by Borough Council on HARB's advice.

Chadds Ford & Birmingham Townships

Chadds Ford's historic district runs along Baltimore Pike from Brandywine Creek. HARB advises the Supervisors.

Birmingham's historic district along Birmingham Road (Dilworthtown) works the same way.

In both districts, no building permit is issued until a Certificate of Appropriateness is approved. We prepare the HARB package.

Tredyffrin Township

Replacing windows without changing the opening needs no permit. Changing the opening or the structure does.

Mapped historic resources go to the Historical Commission.

Rose Valley Borough

Rose Valley began in 1901 as an Arts & Crafts colony, and nearly the whole borough is now a National Register historic district.

Permits go through the Borough Office at Old Mill Lane. We confirm current forms and any historic review before design.

We match wood windows, doors and trim to Arts & Crafts profiles, repairing what can be saved and replicating what can't.

Nether Providence Township (Wallingford)

The Township's Historical Commission comments on demolition and on work within 300 feet of historic sites.

Structural changes, additions and demolition need permits, with two sets of plans.

Swarthmore Borough

Much of Swarthmore's housing dates from the Victorian period through the 1920s, and many homes still have their deep porches, wood windows and original trim.

A Borough building permit covers additions, alterations, roofing, fences, sheds, driveways and demolition. Our window and door work here respects the house's original look.

New Jersey

Haddonfield

Haddonfield set up its historic district in 1971, the second town in New Jersey to do so, after Cape May. It covers about 488 houses, buildings and sites around Kings Highway.

In the district, a Certificate of Appropriateness is required before exterior changes, including roofing, siding and 'any alteration to the exterior appearance.' Paint color and landscaping are exempt.

The Historic Preservation Commission (HPC) reviews the design and the Planning Board approves. We prepare drawings, specs and photos for the application.

The HPC recommends repairing first, and if replacing, using wood windows with true or permanently affixed divided lites, not vinyl or clad. Solid wood entry doors keep their transoms and sidelights.

Porches are 'the focus of historic buildings' in the guidelines: repair before replace.

The Main Street historic area has more than 350 contributing buildings. We check your address.

Repair or replace?

Some boards answer that for you. Haddonfield's HPC recommends repairing wood windows first, and if they're replaced, using wood with true or permanently affixed divided lites. Solid wood entry doors keep their transoms and sidelights, and porches get repaired before they get replaced.

On a historic house we repair what can be saved and replicate what can't, matching the original profiles. In Wayne that means true divided-lite windows where they belong; in Rose Valley, wood windows, doors and trim matched to Arts & Crafts profiles.

And if fixing the old unit is more work than it's worth, we'll tell you. Most window and door problems aren't the product anyway. They're the opening: rot behind the old frame, no flashing, a sill that slopes the wrong way.

Common historic district questions.

Do I need HPC approval to replace windows in Haddonfield?

In the historic district, yes. Exterior changes need a Certificate of Appropriateness. The guidelines favor repairing wood windows, and if they're replaced, using wood with divided lites. We'll assess yours and prepare the application.

Can I put vinyl windows on a house in a historic district?

It depends on the town. Haddonfield's commission expects wood windows in the district, not vinyl or clad. We check the rules for your street before you choose a product.

Do I need HARB approval for windows or a porch in Wayne?

If your home is in the North Wayne, South Wayne or Louella Court historic districts, exterior work goes through Radnor's Historical Architectural Review Board (HARB), which meets the first Wednesday of the month. We'll check your address and tell you up front.

Is my Lower Merion home in a historic district?

Seven local districts are reviewed by HARB, including Gladwyne/Merion Square and Haverford Station. We check your address before design so exterior plans pass review.

Does my Media home need historic approval?

Only if it's in the Courthouse Square, Lemon Street or Providence Friends' Meeting House districts, and only for exterior work visible from the street. We check your address first.

Do I need historic approval before a building permit in Chadds Ford?

If your home is in the Chadds Ford or Birmingham historic district, yes. No building permit is issued until a Certificate of Appropriateness is approved. We prepare the HARB package.

Do window replacements need a permit in Tredyffrin?

Not if the opening stays the same size. Changing the opening or the structure does need a permit.

Is my Moorestown home under the new Historic Preservation Commission?

The HPC, created in 2025, reviews exterior changes in the designated district, which centers on Main Street and Chester Avenue. We confirm your property's status before design.

Rules change, so confirm with your township or borough before you order windows or doors, or have us check it for you. Call (215) 980‑0195. You can also see what we install on our windows & doors page, or read our deck permit guide.
