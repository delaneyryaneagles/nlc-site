Deck Permit Guide

Deck Permits in Delaware County, the Main Line & South Jersey: What You Need

Township by township, the deck and porch rules for the towns we work in.

Google Reviews

Every town does it a little differently.

Updated November 16, 2026

Almost every deck we build needs a permit. What changes from town to town is everything around it: zoning first or building first, how much hard surface you can add before stormwater rules kick in, and whether a historic board has to sign off on a porch.

Below are the rules we've looked up for the townships we work in most, sorted by area. If your town isn't listed, call us and we'll check it for you.

We're licensed in all three states: PA HIC #PA201433, DE #2026700487 and NJ HIC #13VH14325000. Family-run, over 50 years in the trade, and fully insured.

Delaware County & the Main Line, PA

Haverford Township (Havertown)

Every deck needs a building permit.

Contractors must be licensed with the Township and carry workers' comp naming the Township. We're set up.

Additions need a scaled plot plan showing building coverage and impervious-surface %, plus a stormwater site-plan application.

Removing or pruning a public shade tree needs its own permit, and replacements are required.

Official permit page →

Radnor Township (Wayne)

Most construction needs a Radnor permit, and every contractor must be registered with the Township.

Stormwater: roughly 500–999 SF of new impervious surface needs a grading permit, and 1,000 SF or more needs a full stormwater plan. It must be approved before a building permit is accepted.

Historic districts (North Wayne, South Wayne, Louella Court): new construction and additions go through the Historical Architectural Review Board (HARB), which meets the first Wednesday of the month.

Official permit page →

Villanova

Villanova is split between Radnor Township (Delaware County) and Lower Merion Township (Montgomery County). The first step is confirming which side your parcel is on.

Radnor side: grading at about 500 SF and a stormwater plan at 1,000 SF of new hard surface.

Lower Merion side: adding 1,500 SF or less is exempt from peak-rate stormwater control.

Lower Merion Township

Impervious surface: a deck of 200 SF or less doesn't count against your lot. Over 200 SF, half the deck area counts, assuming a porous surface underneath.

Additions need an impervious-surface calculation. More than 1,500 SF of disturbance can require an engineered plan plus an erosion-control permit.

Seven local historic districts (Ardmore Commercial Center, Merion Friends Meeting/General Wayne Inn, Haverford Station, English Village, Harriton, Gladwyne/Merion Square and Mill Creek): HARB reviews visible exterior work.

Residential permits are filed through the Township's online portal. Narberth is its own borough, so permits there go to Narberth Borough Code Enforcement.

Official permit page →

Easttown Township

Decks need zoning and building permits, plus a drainage review if hard surface grows.

Covered porches need zoning, building and drainage permits.

Drainage: 500–999 SF of new hard surface uses a simplified approach; 1,000 SF or more needs an engineered plan.

Tredyffrin Township

Applications are online only, and residential review takes up to 15 business days.

The permit must be posted where it's visible from the street.

Mapped historic resources go to the Historical Commission.

Official permit page →

Chadds Ford & Birmingham Townships

Chadds Ford's historic district runs along Baltimore Pike from Brandywine Creek. HARB advises the Supervisors, and a Certificate of Appropriateness comes before the building permit.

Birmingham's historic district along Birmingham Road (Dilworthtown) works the same way, with the Certificate of Appropriateness first.

Official permit page →

Thornbury Township (Delaware County)

A zoning permit and plot plan go with every building permit.

There are two Thornbury Townships, so we confirm which one is yours.

Upper Providence Township

You need a permit before adding a deck or an extension.

Stormwater permits are separate. Additions generally need sealed architect or engineer plans.

Official permit page →

Media Borough

Three local historic districts: Courthouse Square, Lemon Street and Providence Friends' Meeting House.

In those districts, exterior work visible from the street needs a Certificate of Appropriateness from Borough Council, on HARB's advice.

Middletown Township

Applications are online only (GovWell).

Newtown Township (Delaware County)

Residential review takes up to 15 business days.

Applications need two sets of plans, a signed contract, and insurance with a PA HIC number.

Don't confuse it with Newtown Township in Bucks County; its rules are different.

Official permit page →

Edgmont Township

Sealed plans are required for structural changes and most additions.

Permits take about 1–10 days, and fees double if work starts before the permit is issued. We never start early.

Nether Providence Township (Wallingford)

Structural changes, additions and demolition need permits, with two sets of plans.

The Township's Historical Commission comments on demolition and on work within 300 feet of historic sites.

Official permit page →

South Jersey

Haddonfield

In the historic district, a Certificate of Appropriateness is required before exterior changes, including 'any alteration to the exterior appearance.' Paint color and landscaping are exempt.

The Historic Preservation Commission reviews the design and the Planning Board approves. Its guidelines call porches 'the focus of historic buildings' and favor repair before replacement, without vinyl.

Construction permits follow the NJ Uniform Construction Code: building, electrical, plumbing and fire subcodes as needed.

Official permit page →

Moorestown Township

Decks typically need zoning approval first, then a building permit (plus electrical if the deck is lit).

Plans come from a NJ licensed design professional where required.

A Historic Preservation Commission, created in 2025, reviews exterior changes in its district, mainly Main Street and Chester Avenue.

Official permit page →

Harrison Township (Mullica Hill)

Decks and porches start with a zoning application, then a construction permit with drawings (filed in person or by mail).

A final inspection happens before your final payment.

The historic Mullica Hill district along Main Street needs Historic Preservation Commission approval for exterior work.

Official permit page →

Woolwich Township

For decks, siding, basements and additions, the first step is a call to the Construction Office.

Medford Township

UCC permits are required, and Medford requires HIC registration with every residential permit. Ours is 13VH14325000.

About 78% of the township is in the state Pinelands Area, where some development needs a Pinelands Commission filing.

Lake communities may have their own HOA or lake-club approvals. Medford Lakes is a separate borough with its own offices.

Official permit page →

Delaware

New Castle County (Greenville, Centreville, Hockessin)

Only small, low, detached decks (200 SF or less and 30 in or lower) are exempt.

Every other deck needs plans showing footings, framing and rails, plus a sealed plot plan.

Greenville, Centreville and Hockessin are unincorporated, so the County issues permits through eApply/ePlans.

Historic overlay parcels need Historic Review Board approval before any exterior permit.

Common permit questions.

Do I need a permit to build a deck?

Almost always. Haverford Township requires a building permit for every deck, Easttown requires zoning and building permits, and Upper Providence asks for a permit before adding a deck. In New Castle County, DE, only small, low, detached decks are exempt. We pull the permit and schedule the inspections.

How big can my deck be before it counts as impervious in Lower Merion?

Decks of 200 SF or less don't count against your lot. Above that, half the deck area counts, assuming a porous surface underneath. We size the deck to make the most of it.

Will a new deck trigger stormwater rules in Radnor?

It can. Radnor counts new hard surface: roughly 500 to 999 SF needs a grading permit, and 1,000 SF or more needs a full stormwater management plan, approved before a building permit is accepted.

Is any deck exempt from a permit in New Castle County, DE?

Only small, low, detached decks: 200 SF or less and 30 in or lower. Other decks need plans showing footings, framing and rails, plus a sealed plot plan.

Do I need zoning approval for a deck in South Jersey?

In Harrison Township (Mullica Hill), decks and porches start with a zoning application, then the construction permit. In Moorestown, decks typically need zoning first. In Woolwich, the first step is a call to the Construction Office.

Do I need historic approval for a porch?

In some districts, yes. In Chadds Ford and Birmingham, a Certificate of Appropriateness comes before the building permit. Haddonfield's historic district needs one for exterior changes, and in Media's three districts it covers exterior work visible from the street. We check your address first.

Which township handles permits for my Villanova home?

Villanova is split between Radnor Township and Lower Merion Township, and their stormwater rules differ. We look up your parcel before design so we follow the right rules from day one.

What happens if work starts before the permit?

In Edgmont Township, permit fees double if work starts before the permit is issued. We never start early.

Rules change, so confirm with your township before you build, or have us check it for you. Call (215) 980‑0195. You can also see what we build on our decks & railings page.
